Philosophy Experience & CV

Core BeliefWhy Physics, Statistics and Uncertainty Matter

The world does not give us certainty — it gives us signals, noise, and incomplete information. My career has been built on the conviction that: the honest quantification of uncertainty is the foundation of every trustworthy decision.

Physics provides the mechanistic language. Statistics provides the framework for reasoning under uncertainty. Probabilistic machine learning and Bayesian decision-making bridge the two, letting data and domain knowledge speak together rather than compete

When the consequences of getting it wrong are measured in human lives, environmental damage, or billions of dollars, there is no room for overconfidence. There is only room for rigour, transparency, and the courage to say “we don’t know — and this is how uncertain we are.”

PracticeDevOps is Applied Scientific Method

Science is the gold standard for building lasting knowledge: observe carefully, state assumptions, test them, and let new evidence change your mind. In Bayesian terms, learning means updating beliefs honestly rather than defending yesterday’s certainty.

DevOps, MLOps, and disciplined engineering practices carry that standard into software, AI, and machine learning. When models influence important decisions, validation, monitoring, traceability, and controlled change are not bureaucracy — they are how teams preserve knowledge, improve judgment, and adapt responsibly.

Good systems do not just scale execution; they scale learning.

01

Version Everything

Code, data, models, prompts, configurations, and decision rules — if it changes, it lives in version control.

02

Automate Validation

Automated validation in CI/CD pipelines is the peer review of any code-based process, including software, AI, and machine learning, and should be built into every path to deployment.

03

Make Failure Visible

Monitoring and model drift detection surface problems before decisions are corrupted.

04

Traceability Over Guesswork

Probabilistic systems will not always replay identical outcomes, but the workflow, assumptions, data lineage, and decision process should always be auditable and reproducible in spirit.

Human FactorsThe Buddy Check: From the Mountains and the Deep

"No heroes. Check each other, trust each other, go home safe — together."

In climbing and scuba diving, the buddy check is non-negotiable. Before every climb, before every descent, your partner checks your gear and you check theirs. It is a ritual built on one truth: humans make mistakes, and two sets of eyes are exponentially more reliable than one.

This practice has profoundly shaped how I approach engineering, data science, and leadership:

  • Peer review is not a hurdle — it is a lifeline. The goal is to catch errors before they compound into consequences.
  • Shared responsibility removes single points of failure. When ownership is distributed, knowledge is distributed.
  • Psychological safety enables honest checks. A buddy check only works if both people feel safe to speak up.
  • The goal is team success, not individual heroics. Celebrate collective wins. No room for lone wolves.

Challenge Networks

Deliberately cultivate colleagues whose job is constructive disagreement. Surrounding yourself only with agreement is the fastest route to confident mistakes.

Intention-Based Leadership

Give teams the context — the why and the intent — then trust them to determine the how. Micromanagement creates compliance; intent creates ownership.
